Call for Papers

The STRIDE workshop, co-located with EuroSys 2027, brings together researchers and practitioners working on the design and operation of stable, reliable, and fault-tolerant AI infrastructures to discuss the next steps in AI infrastructure evolution.

Large-scale AI infrastructures are an integral part of modern data centers. Training and inference require elastic fault tolerance mechanisms that adapt to workload changes and unexpected faults while maintaining continuous operation and minimizing performance degradation. New approaches at both software and hardware levels are needed to connect reliability mechanisms across the stack.

The workshop focuses on training and inference stability, numerical robustness, cost-effective checkpointing and elastic reconfiguration, inference and agent service continuity under failures, and software/hardware co-designed reliability. Resilient agent infrastructures, rapid recovery, and reliable communication between heterogeneous CPUs and xPUs are central challenges.

We welcome contributions from academia and industry addressing full-stack reliability for large-scale AI training, inference, and distributed agent services. Production experience reports and evaluations connecting infrastructure failures to convergence, model quality, and service-level objectives are particularly encouraged.

STRIDE builds on the AIOps workshop series held in 2021, 2022, and 2023 in the context of ICDM and ICSOC. The new format focuses on reliable and stable AI infrastructures and connects this discussion to the systems research community at EuroSys.

A full-day workshop with two keynote talks, five research presentations selected from submissions, and a panel discussion with five experts from academia and industry. The workshop provides an interactive platform for discussing current challenges and advances in AI reliability and fault tolerance.

Topics of Interest

We invite contributions on the following topics, including but not limited to:

  • AI training and inference stability challenges
  • Root cause analysis, explainability, and mitigation approaches for instability
  • Numerical training robustness
  • Detection, localization, and mitigation of silent data corruption
  • Cost-effective checkpointing, localized recovery, and elastic reconfiguration
  • Inference and agent service continuity under failures
  • Production experience reports and evaluation methods linking infrastructure faults to training convergence, model quality, and SLOs
  • Software/hardware co-designed reliability
  • Large-scale distributed agent infrastructure reliability

Contributions may address loss oscillations, convergence problems, norm spikes, error accumulation, and the interplay between numerical errors and hardware faults. Further areas include recovery of KV caches, agent state and sandbox environments, fault localization, self-healing, and reliable memory for long-horizon tasks.

Submission Details

We invite papers of up to 6 pages in double-column ACM format, excluding the bibliography. Papers must use the latest ACM sigconf proceedings template and must be anonymized.

Submission link: To be announced.

Submissions meeting the anonymization and layout requirements will be reviewed by three reviewers. The five best-ranked papers will be selected for presentation. Research talks are planned for 20 minutes, followed by 10 minutes for Q&A and speaker switch.

At least one author of each accepted paper must attend and present the paper for inclusion in the proceedings. Accepted and presented papers are planned to be published in the ACM Digital Library.
